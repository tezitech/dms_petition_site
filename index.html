The Project

Daggett Montessori has always been a K-8 Montessori school. It was moved from its previous, larger building to this campus, the former De Zavala Elementary, which was designed only for grades K-5. The renovation is funded by the FWISD 2021 Bond as the project “Daggett Montessori at New Site.” The district's construction documents title it “FWISD Daggett Montessori School Relocation” and describe the work as “renovation to the existing school for conversion to K-8th grade school.”

Project budget

about $29.9M

Construction contract budget: $15M, including a $750K owner contingency.

Design & management

DLR Group

Architect. Owner's representative: PROCEDEO. Bid: CSP #27-015 (TEA #194).

Contract decision

Feb 9, 2027

Contractor proposals are due and opened Dec 10, 2026, at 2:00 PM. The bid documents point to the Feb 9, 2027 board meeting.

Key dates

  1. Dec 10, 2026

    Contractor proposals due and opened

  2. Feb 9, 2027

    Board meeting where the contract is expected. This is the decision we're asking to pause.

  3. By July 31, 2027

    Phase 1: grading, detention pond, and both parking lots. Construction begins at the end of the 2026-27 school year.

Construction phases

  1. Phase 1 (by July 31, 2027): exterior flatwork, grading, the detention pond, and both parking lots. The phasing plan says its goal is “to create a parking area for staff and students so the existing parking area… can be blocked off for construction activity.”
  2. Phase 2 (by May 31, 2028): the building addition, parking lot revisions, and the administration build-out, with possible window replacement. Classrooms move into temporary buildings on site while their areas are under construction.
  3. Phase 3: interior build-outs and re-roofing, ending at substantial completion on June 23, 2028.

Addendum 2 calls the phase dates “for reference” and says they “may be adjusted slightly based on construction timelines.”

The Plan Doesn't Fit a K-8 Montessori

Daggett Montessori has always been K-8, but its new home was designed only for a K-5 elementary. The renovation largely re-labels that building instead of designing it for a K-8 Montessori program. Parking is the most visible symptom, but the larger problem is the project itself.

  1. 1

    A K-8 school in a building designed for K-5

    Daggett Montessori moved here from a larger building. Rooms are re-labeled for K-8 with new casework, sinks, and boards, but no classrooms are added and room sizes mostly stay the same. Montessori multi-age rooms typically need more space.

  2. 2

    Middle school is thin

    One 6th-grade room, four grade 7-8 rooms, and one science lab for all of grades 6-8.

  3. 3

    The Montessori program wasn't the starting point

    Montessori education depends on its environment: right-sized multi-age classrooms, outdoor classrooms, gardens, practical-life work, and land-based learning for grades 7-8. The plan doesn't start from those needs.

  4. 4

    The plan isn't finished

    New landscape areas on the civil drawings are labeled “Material TBD,” and neither addendum reissued those sheets. In Addendum 2, the district says the scope of each phase “is loosely defined” and “will be finalized following the selection of a contractor.” Bidder questions about the schedule, fencing, and science-room gas piping were pushed to Addendum 3.

  5. 5

    Parking paves learning space

    A 34-space lot and a roughly 5,400 sq ft detention pond go on the school's south green space. Other options, such as a shared-parking agreement with the church across College Ave (within the city's 500-ft off-site limit) and keeping part of the existing Alston parking, are available.

  6. 6

    Once the contract is signed, the money is committed

    The $15M construction contract is about half of the roughly $29.9M project budget. Getting the plan right before award is far easier than changing course after construction starts.

What the Current Plan Buys

Many of these upgrades are needed. Our concern is that they're designed around a K-5 elementary layout rather than a K-8 Montessori program. Families and teachers should help decide how this investment is spent.

Classrooms
  • Rooms re-labeled for K-8, with new casework, sinks, and boards.
  • No classrooms are added, and room sizes mostly stay the same.
Science
  • One science classroom for grades 6-8.
  • A new prep room of about 260 sq ft.
Arts
  • Auditorium acoustic upgrades and wheelchair seating.
  • The existing stage, music room, and two art rooms stay.
Front office & security
  • New front-office addition of about 2,100 sq ft.
  • Secure entry vestibule with bullet-resistant glass.
  • Card readers and a new intercom.
  • New 6-foot ornamental security fence around the campus.
Finishes
  • New flooring, paint, and ceilings.
Restrooms
  • Fixtures and partitions replaced.
Accessibility
  • Lifts, rails, and accessible routes.
Building systems
  • Steam heat replaced with new heating and cooling.
  • New lighting, fire sprinklers, and fire alarm.
  • New windows, with the historic look kept on the front.
Site & parking
  • Parking Lot A: 34 spaces on the south green space along Alston Ave.
  • A dry detention pond of roughly 5,400 sq ft to hold runoff from the new paving.
  • Parking Lot B: 20 spaces, rebuilding the existing lot across Alston Ave.
  • The existing parking strip along Alston inside the main campus is removed for the addition and new landscaping.

What We're Asking For

  1. Pause the contract. Postpone the Board of Managers vote on the Daggett Montessori construction contract (the bid documents point to the Feb 9, 2027 board meeting) until a comprehensive plan is in place.
  2. Build a comprehensive K-8 Montessori plan first. Develop the project requirements with the Daggett Montessori PTA, teachers and school leadership, Montessori specialists, and neighbors, covering classrooms, middle school, science, outdoor learning, and parking and traffic.
  3. Make that plan the basis of the proposal. Revise the design and the contractor proposal to match it, so the bond dollars fund what a K-8 Montessori program actually needs.
  4. Solve parking without paving learning space. Evaluate a shared-parking agreement with the neighboring church and keeping part of the existing Alston parking as part of the plan.

We are not asking FWISD to cancel its investment in Daggett Montessori. We also aren't asking the district to drop the parking lot and build everything else as drawn. We want the whole project planned around the school it serves before the money is committed.
